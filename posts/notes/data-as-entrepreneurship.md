At the 2022 ICSB World Congress, I shared my journey through GWU’s Master of Science in Business Analytics, laying bare how it reshaped my view of entrepreneurship’s future. ([Watch the talk on YouTube.](https://youtu.be/alorMj-GUjk))

I spoke of a pattern I’ve seen in organizations I’ve worked with: they’re swimming in data, often collecting it diligently, but they’re stumped on what it can do or how to unlock its worth. It’s like holding a toolbox without knowing which tool fits the job.

This isn’t just a quirk—it’s a signal of a wider shift, one that entrepreneurs might seize. But first, a question: Are you, or your outfit, squeezing every drop from your data? How would you prove it?

I’d wager most should say no. Entrepreneurs, by nature, see untapped potential; they’re wired to spot gaps. Data scientists, too, should lean toward no, driven by a relentless itch to probe deeper.

Curiosity, creativity, inventiveness—these aren’t so far apart. My talk hinged on this convergence, less about splitting hairs over definitions and more about what binds them: a drive to make something new from what’s at hand.

I closed with this: data science is entrepreneurship in disguise. The act of turning raw data into something valuable tracks the entrepreneur’s path—methodical yet bold, structured yet free.

With data and tech racing forward, those who can straddle the line between scientific precision and leadership grit will shape what’s next. They’ll build not just businesses, but new ways of seeing.
